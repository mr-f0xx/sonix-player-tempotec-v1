Live USB remote

This controls a physical V1, not the emulator. Enable Developer options → ADB on the player, leave its microSD inserted, and connect with a USB data cable. Open this site directly in a WebUSB-capable browser. On Android, use USB host/OTG.

Not connected. The editors also work offline.

Parametric EQ

Select a band, then drag on the chart to set frequency and gain. Use the fields below for precise values. The preview uses 48 kHz and does not change playback.

Saved to PEQ/. Select the new preset on the player to apply it.

Tape Deck pad bank

Choose local PCM WAVs for any of the 16 pads. Move samples between slots with the arrows; empty slots stay empty.

On the V1, open Tape Deck → Pads → Kits → Load drum kit. This creates a kit, not a saved sequence.

Playlist editor

Edit one track path per line. Use paths relative to the card root, such as Music/Album/Track.flac. Saving creates a new import file, not an edit to the V1 library database.

Use Playlists → Backup on the V1 to expose an existing library playlist, or Import to add a saved file to your library.

Quick Test Ideas

  • Open Music → TempoTec Demo and tap a track (enable Audio: On in the top bar to hear real PCM output!).
  • Switch between the 44.1 kHz and 96 kHz demo tracks to see the real SGM31324 RGB LED change color & the Hi-Res badge update.
  • Click Hold Power (2s) or hold the side PWR button to summon the V1 Power Menu.
  • Slide Battery to 15% or switch Charger to Charging and watch both the topbar icon and the breathing RGB LED respond.
  • Try Settings → Buttons to test hardware button remapping, or Settings → Screen → Rotate 180°.

Video for TempoTec V1

The Ingenic X1600 has no hardware H.264/HEVC video decoder. Sonix plays video by software-decoding MPEG-1 video + MP2 audio in an MPEG-PS .mpg container at the native 320×240 landscape resolution (rotated to the 240×320 panel during playback).

One-Click ffmpeg Converter

Convert any video to a V1-friendly .mpg. Requires ffmpeg installed on your computer.

Recommended (320×240, 24 fps, good quality):
ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -vf "scale=320:240:force_original_aspect_ratio=decrease,pad=320:240:(ow-iw)/2:(oh-ih)/2,fps=24" -c:v mpeg1video -q:v 6 -c:a mp2 -b:a 128k -ar 44100 -ac 2 output.mpg
Smaller file (lower bitrate):
ffmpeg -i input.mp4 -vf "scale=320:240:force_original_aspect_ratio=decrease,pad=320:240:(ow-iw)/2:(oh-ih)/2,fps=20" -c:v mpeg1video -b:v 600k -c:a mp2 -b:a 96k -ar 44100 -ac 2 output.mpg
  • Copy the resulting .mpg to your microSD card and open it from Files.
  • Video plays in landscape (320×240). Double-tap the picture to switch to portrait; drag the bar on the panel edge to scrub.
  • Next/Previous seek ±30s and Play/Pause pauses; the volume keys keep their usual job.
  • Keep bitrates modest (≤1.5 Mbps video) for smooth software decode on the ~1 GHz MIPS CPU.
  • The video player itself needs the real panel framebuffer, so it runs on the V1 and not in this emulator.
